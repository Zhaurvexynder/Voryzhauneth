# Voryzhauneth

> A glass orchard bears the sound of fruit that no hand has gathered.

## The Orchard Beyond the Rain

The orchard lies beyond a rainstorm that has remained in the same place for eleven generations. To reach it, travelers walk through the rain without becoming wet. Their clothes remain dry, but a room from their childhood fills slowly with water. Most travelers turn back when they hear furniture floating.

The trees are glass. Their roots enter the earth like frozen lightning, and their branches carry pale fruit shaped like closed eyes. No leaves grow between the fruit. Instead, thin ribbons hang from the twigs, each tied around a small absence that cannot be seen directly.

The gardener lives in a house made of ordinary timber. This is considered the most troubling feature of the orchard. He has never opened its front door. He enters through the reflection of a window at sunset and emerges through the same reflection before dawn, holding a bucket of darkness.

## A Season With No Weather

Nothing ripens here. Nothing remains unripe. The fruit is described as ready, but no one has learned what it is ready for.

When wind passes through the trees, the fruit produces separate notes. These notes sometimes assemble into the sound of a familiar name. Those who hear their own names discover a second shadow at their feet. The additional shadow belongs to a child, regardless of the listener's age.

The gardener waters the roots with darkness from his bucket. The darkness flows uphill. At the tips of the branches it becomes transparent, and briefly reveals small figures seated inside the fruit. Each figure is holding a cup against a wall, listening to something on the other side.

In the house, there are eleven chairs and no table. One chair is carved from amber. The others are made from a wood that has no grain. The gardener moves the amber chair once a year, though no year has been observed beginning or ending.

## The Visitor With a Borrowed Hand

A traveler once arrived carrying a hand wrapped in linen. It was his own hand, borrowed from a portrait painted before he was born. He wished to return it, but the portrait had disappeared from the room in which it would eventually hang.

The gardener offered him a place among the trees. The traveler walked between the roots until he found a fruit containing an empty chair. He unwrapped the hand and held it against the glass. Inside, the chair turned slightly toward him.

Neither man spoke. The rain beyond the orchard paused halfway to the ground. In the distant childhood room, the furniture settled back onto the floor. The traveler felt someone remove a ring from a finger he had never possessed.

He left without the borrowed hand. His portrait was later found in a house belonging to no member of his family. It showed the orchard in summer, though the orchard has never admitted to a season.

## What Sleeps Among the Roots

The glass roots surround a hollow too broad to cross in a single lifetime. Something scaled lies within it, arranged in a spiral. No observer has seen its beginning or its end. The roots touch its back without piercing it.

Every scale contains a reflection of the orchard under different rain. In one reflection the rain falls upward. In another it falls as sand. In a third, the trees are dark and a woman sits in the gardener's house, counting the chairs with her eyes closed.

The creature does not wake when the fruit sounds. It does not wake when visitors speak their names. It moves only when someone remembers a thing that never happened. Then a single scale turns, and the orchard becomes silent until the false memory is forgotten.

The gardener keeps no records of these silences. He marks them instead by tying another ribbon around another absence. Some branches carry so many ribbons that their glass bends like a bow beneath invisible snow.

## Words Inside a Broken Note

A ribbon was once untied by accident. What it contained was a note too narrow for human hearing. The note broke against the visitor's teeth. Four fragments remained on the tongue:

*Vor — yzha — uneth — sael.*

The fragments were placed in a jar. At night, they arranged themselves into the shape of a key. By morning, the key had become a small staircase. The jar was eventually returned to the orchard, where it now hangs from a branch that nobody can locate twice.

The visitor lived for many years afterward. Whenever he laughed, someone in a distant village found a glass seed in a loaf of bread. Nobody planted these seeds. Nevertheless, a second orchard appeared beneath the first, visible only in puddles.

## The Last Chair

There is a custom among those who return: they leave one chair empty at every meal. The empty place is given no plate, no glass, and no explanation. Children sometimes look toward it before answering a question.

The gardener denies teaching this custom. He denies having visitors. He also denies being the gardener, but continues to carry the bucket every morning.

One evening, all eleven chairs were seen through the window. On each sat a figure with a cup held against its chest. The house remained silent. Beneath the orchard, a scale turned without disturbing the roots.

For the first time, a fruit fell.

It made no sound when it touched the ground. Instead, somewhere beyond the rain, a locked door opened into a room that had always been dry.

The fallen fruit remains where it landed.

The empty chair has not been found.
